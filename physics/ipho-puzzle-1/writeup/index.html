Writeup · IPhO Puzzle 1

The Vanishing Contact

Official writeup for the problem posted 20 August 2026. The statement is separate, so a solver can sit the problem first.

This page is the solution. If you have not yet tried the problem, open the statement instead.

Reusable idea

A contact constraint survives only while the constraint force has a physically allowed sign. For a bead on the outside of a sphere, \(N \ge 0\). The sphere can push outward, cannot pull inward.

Part A — Velocity

Energy. The drop in height from the top is

\[ \Delta h = R(1-\cos\theta). \]

Mechanical energy is conserved:

\[ mgR(1-\cos\theta)=\tfrac12 mv^2. \]

Hence

\[ v^2=2gR(1-\cos\theta), \] \[ v(\theta)=\sqrt{2gR(1-\cos\theta)}. \]

Part B — The moment of departure

Radial equation, toward the center taken positive:

\[ mg\cos\theta - N = \frac{mv^2}{R}. \]

The normal force is therefore

\[ N = mg\cos\theta - \frac{mv^2}{R}. \]

Substitute the energy result:

\[ N = mg(3\cos\theta-2). \]

\(N=0\) marks departure because \(N<0\) would require the sphere to pull. Set \(3\cos\theta_0-2=0\):

\[ \cos\theta_0=\frac23,\qquad \theta_0=\cos^{-1}\!\left(\frac23\right)\approx 48.2^\circ. \]

The speed at that instant is

\[ v_0^2=\frac{2gR}{3},\qquad v_0=\sqrt{\frac{2gR}{3}}. \]

Also, at departure \(N=0\), so \(v_0^2=gR\cos\theta_0\).

Part C — The trajectory after departure

Origin at the center. The departure coordinates are

\[ x_0=R\sin\theta_0=\frac{\sqrt5}{3}R,\qquad y_0=\frac23 R. \]

The unit tangent is \((\cos\theta,\,-\sin\theta)\). The velocity components at departure are

\[ v_{x0}=v_0\cos\theta_0=\frac23\sqrt{\frac{2gR}{3}}, \] \[ v_{y0}=-v_0\sin\theta_0=-\frac{\sqrt5}{3}\sqrt{\frac{2gR}{3}}. \]

After loss of contact, gravity only:

\[ x(t)=\frac{\sqrt5}{3}R + \frac23\sqrt{\frac{2gR}{3}}\, t, \] \[ y(t)=\frac23 R - \frac{\sqrt5}{3}\sqrt{\frac{2gR}{3}}\, t - \tfrac12 gt^2. \]

Part D — Where does it hit the ground?

The floor is at \(y_{\mathrm{floor}}=-R\). Introduce the dimensionless time \(s=t_f\sqrt{g/R}\). The impact condition produces the quadratic

\[ 9s^2+2\sqrt{30}\,s-30=0. \]

The physical root is

\[ s=\frac{10\sqrt3-\sqrt{30}}{9}. \]

The horizontal distance from the point where the sphere touches the floor is

\[ D=\frac{5(\sqrt5+4\sqrt2)}{27}\,R. \]

Thus \(C=5(\sqrt5+4\sqrt2)/27\approx 1.462\).

Gold-medal extension

Write \(\alpha=u^2/(gR)\). Energy now gives

\[ v^2=gR\bigl[\alpha+2(1-\cos\theta)\bigr]. \]

The normal force becomes

\[ N=mg(3\cos\theta-\alpha-2), \]

and in particular

\[ N(0)=mg(1-\alpha). \]

Regime I — \(0\le\alpha<1\)

The bead remains in contact at the top, then leaves where

\[ \cos\theta_0=\frac{\alpha+2}{3},\qquad v_0^2=\frac{\alpha+2}{3}\,gR. \]

\(\alpha=0\) is the original problem.

Regime II — \(\alpha=1\)

\(N=0\) at the top, so the bead loses contact immediately. The critical value is \(\alpha_c=1\).

Regime III — \(\alpha>1\)

\(N(0)<0\): there is no sustained contact, and the motion is an immediate projectile. For \(\alpha\ge 1\) and \(t>0\),

\[ r^2-R^2=gR(\alpha-1)t^2+\tfrac14 g^2 t^4 > 0. \]

Reusable Olympiad trick

Assume the constraint is active; derive the required force; ask whether that force has an allowed sign; find the boundary; verify that continuation would require a forbidden force.

A surface requires \(N\ge 0\). A loose string requires \(T\ge 0\). A rigid rod can push and pull, so \(F=0\) does not generally mean the constraint disappears.

Mastery Check 1A — The Vertical Loop

A follow-on exercise, not IPhO Puzzle 2.

A small block of mass \(m\) is launched from the bottom of the inside of a smooth vertical circular track of radius \(R\) with speed \(V\). It is not attached; the track can push, cannot pull. Let \(\theta\) be measured from the bottom, and write \(\beta=V^2/(gR)\). Classify the motion for all \(\beta\ge 0\):

  1. \(v(\theta)\) while contact persists.
  2. \(N(\theta)\).
  3. The minimum \(\beta\) to complete the entire loop while maintaining contact.
  4. If it loses contact before completing the loop, the angle.
  5. The range of launch speeds where it does not lose contact — it runs out of kinetic energy and slides back. That range.
  6. Borderline cases; two especially important critical values of \(\beta\).

Mastery sentence. What quantity must I calculate to decide whether the assumed circular constraint remains physically possible, and what inequality must that quantity obey?
