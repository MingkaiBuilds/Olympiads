IPhO Puzzle 1 — The Vanishing Contact

Posted 20 August 2026 (America/New_York). The statement is below. There are no hints on this page.

  • Topic Mechanics — energy, constraints, normal forces
  • Pattern to learn The instant a constraint disappears, the constraint force is zero.
  • Target time 35 minutes
  • Difficulty ★★★☆☆ initially; the final part is the real test.
  • Track Physics / IPhO
  • Writeup Published separately

Rules. No hints on this page. Treat the problem as competition conditions. Use only classical mechanics.

A small bead of mass \(m\) is released from rest at the top of a smooth fixed sphere of radius \(R\). It slides down the outside of the sphere under gravity.

Let \(\theta\) denote the angle measured from the upward vertical through the center of the sphere.

Air resistance is negligible.

Part A — Velocity

While the bead remains in contact with the sphere, derive its speed \(v(\theta)\).

Part B — The moment of departure

The bead eventually loses contact with the sphere.

  1. Find the angle \(\theta_0\) at which this occurs.
  2. Find its speed \(v_0\) at that instant.

You must justify physically why your mathematical condition corresponds to loss of contact.

Part C — The trajectory after departure

Take the center of the sphere as the origin, with \(x\) horizontal and \(y\) vertically upward.

Derive parametric equations \(x(t)\), \(y(t)\) for the bead after it leaves the sphere, where \(t=0\) is the instant of departure.

Part D — Where does it hit the ground?

The sphere rests on a horizontal floor, so its center is a height \(R\) above the floor.

Determine the horizontal distance \(D\), measured from the point where the sphere touches the floor, at which the bead first strikes the floor.

Express the answer as \(D = C R\) and determine the exact value of \(C\).

Gold-medal extension

Instead of being released from rest at the top, the bead is launched horizontally along the sphere from the top with speed \(u\).

Define \(\alpha = u^2/(gR)\).

Classify all physically distinct regimes as \(\alpha\) varies.

In particular, determine: whether the bead initially remains in contact; if so, where it loses contact; and the critical value(s) of \(\alpha\) separating qualitatively different behavior.

A complete answer should explain the physics of every regime, not merely produce formulas.

Central question. How do I mathematically detect when a physical constraint can no longer enforce the motion I assumed?
