Writeup · IMO Puzzle 1

Number Theory

Official writeup for the problem posted 20 August 2026. The statement is separate, so a solver can sit the problem first.

This page is the solution. If you have not yet tried the problem, open the statement instead.

We prove that if \(\dfrac{a^2+b^2}{ab+1}=k\in\mathbb{Z}\), then \(k\) is a perfect square. That is,

\[ a^2+b^2=k(ab+1). \tag{1} \]

The central idea is Vieta jumping.

1. A minimal counterexample

Assume a counterexample exists: \(k\) is not a square. Among positive integer solutions \((a,b)\) giving a nonsquare \(k\), minimize \(a+b\). By symmetry, take \(a\ge b\). Then \(k\ge 2\).

If \(b=1\), then \(a^2+1=k(a+1)\), so \(a+1\) divides \(a^2+1\). Since \(a^2+1=(a+1)(a-1)+2\), we have \(a+1\mid 2\). With \(a\ge 1\) this forces \(a=1\) and \(k=1\), a square. Contradiction. Thus \(b\ge 2\).

2. The other root

The equation is quadratic in \(a\):

\[ a^2 - kba + (b^2-k)=0. \]

Write \(f(x)=x^2-kbx+(b^2-k)\). One root is \(x=a\). Call the other root \(c\).

Vieta: \(a+c=kb\), so \(c=kb-a\in\mathbb{Z}\). The product is

\[ ac=b^2-k. \tag{2} \]

We need \(0<c<b\).

3. Prove \(c>0\)

If \(b^2=k\), then \(k\) is a square, a contradiction. So \(b^2\neq k\).

Suppose \(b^2<k\). Then \(ac<0\). Since \(a>0\), we have \(c<0\). Write \(c=-d\) with \(d\ge 1\). Then

\[ k=ad+b^2 \ge a+b^2 > a. \tag{3} \]

But

\[ k=\frac{a^2+b^2}{ab+1} < \frac{a^2+b^2}{ab} = \frac{a}{b}+\frac{b}{a}. \]

Since \(b\ge 2\) and \(a\ge b\), we have \(a/b \le a/2\) and \(b/a \le 1\), so

\[ k < \tfrac{a}{2}+1 \le a. \tag{4} \]

(3) and (4) contradict. Therefore \(b^2>k\), hence \(ac>0\). With \(a>0\) we get \(c>0\).

4. Prove \(c<b\)

\(f(0)=b^2-k>0\). Also \(f(b)=(2-k)b^2-k\). Since \(k\ge 2\), we have \(f(b)<0\). So one root lies strictly between \(0\) and \(b\). The roots are \(a\) and \(c\), and \(a\ge b\), so \(0<c<b\).

5. The jump

Since \(c\) is a root, \(b^2+c^2=k(bc+1)\). Thus \((b,c)\) is another positive integer solution for the same nonsquare \(k\). From \(0<c<b\le a\) we have \(b+c < a+b\), contradicting minimality of \(a+b\).

Therefore no nonsquare \(k\) exists. QED.

Lesson

Diophantine + quadratic + minimality \(\Rightarrow\) descent.

Reusable sequence: equation \(F(a,b,k)=0\); minimize a bad solution; order \(a\ge b\); monic quadratic in the larger variable; Vieta other root; prove it is integer, positive, and smaller; replace \((a,b)\to(b,c)\); contradict minimality. The olympiad work is proving \(0<c<b\).

Mastery Problem 1A

A follow-on exercise, not IMO Puzzle 2. No solution is given here.

Let \(a\), \(b\) be positive integers such that \(ab\) divides \(a^2+b^2+1\). Prove that \(\dfrac{a^2+b^2+1}{ab}=3\). Equivalently, if \(a^2+b^2+1=kab\), prove that \(k=3\).

Do not search for another technique. The proof must identify: the minimal object; the quadratic; the second root; why it is an integer; why it is positive; why it is strictly smaller; what the descent reaches; and why the terminal case forces \(k=3\).
