IMO Puzzle 1 — Number Theory

Posted 20 August 2026 (America/New_York). The statement is below. There is no writeup on this site.

  • Weapon Vieta jumping
  • Time target 60 minutes
  • Track Mathematics / IMO
  • Writeup Not yet earned

Let \(a\), \(b\) be positive integers such that \(\dfrac{a^2 + b^2}{ab + 1}\) is an integer.

Prove that this integer is a perfect square.

Equivalently, if \(a^2 + b^2 = k(ab + 1)\) for some positive integer \(k\), prove that \(k = n^2\) for some integer \(n\).

Rules for this attempt. Do not brute-force it computationally. Small examples by hand are allowed. The objective is a proof.

First attack

The first attack should answer:

  1. What can you learn from small solutions?
  2. Suppose a counterexample exists. Can you choose a minimal counterexample?
  3. If you treat \(x^2 - kbx + (b^2 - k) = 0\) as a quadratic whose root is \(x = a\), what can you say about its other root?
  4. Could that other root produce a smaller positive integer solution?

Today’s lesson

A recurring IMO pattern is: Diophantine equation + quadratic structure + minimality \(\Rightarrow\) descent.

When an integer equation is quadratic in one variable, look for the other root. Vieta’s formulas can secretly generate another integer solution. If it is smaller, minimality can destroy the supposed counterexample.

This technique is called Vieta jumping.

Knowing the name isn’t solving it. The hard part is proving the new root is positive and smaller, then understanding what happens at the bottom of the descent.
